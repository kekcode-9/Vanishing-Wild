What the occurrence density map tells us:
1. Each point depicted in the GBIF occurrence density map represents a recorded occurrence or observation of a species at 
a specific geographic location. Specifically:

2. A point corresponds to an individual occurrence report in the GBIF database, which may come from published 
specimen collections, human observations, or sensor data.

3. Each occurrence includes metadata such as date, collector, identification, and coordinates.

4. The density map aggregates how many such occurrence records fall within a spatial grid cell or tile at a given zoom level.

5. It does not represent population size or abundance, only where and how many records have been submitted.

6. Due to biases in sampling effort (geographic bias, accessibility, observer density), these points and their density may not 
perfectly reflect the true species distribution or abundance.

7. It provides a visual proxy for species presence data accumulated over time from multiple sources.

8. In summary, each point on the density map is a documented species observation or specimen record location present in GBIF’s 
aggregated data. The map tiles show the spatial density of these points to visualize distributions and sampling effort levels.

With the GBIF occurrence density map, zooming in to a high level of detail only reveals the 
tile-level density but does NOT show you actual individual observation points—because:

The density raster tiles summarize the number of occurrences per grid cell for a given zoom, not actual point locations.

Even at maximum zoom, you are still seeing aggregated record counts, not fine-grained individual observation markers.

When Should You Zoom?
Lower zoom levels (zoomed out): Best for understanding overall species distribution, global/regional “hotspots,” 
and gaps in sampling.

Higher zoom levels (zoomed in): Good for exploring regional variation, or checking the density of records 
in more specific local regions. But you won’t see true individual point locations, only finer aggregation.

If your goal is to examine actual individual occurrences (the true latitude/longitude for each record), 
you should use the GBIF Occurrence API’s /occurrence/search endpoint with hasCoordinate=true, and plot those explicit 
point locations on your own map layer.
